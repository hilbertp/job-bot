Dear BnBerry team,

your posting is unusually honest about what the job actually is: not inventing a platform, but keeping a complex, integration-heavy one healthy while growing what it delivers. I find that framing refreshing, because stewardship of an existing system is harder than greenfield and rarely gets written down as the actual mandate. It is also the work I have done for most of a decade.

Every platform I have run, I inherited. At EMIL I took over a stalled multi-tenant B2B SaaS platform and a 17-person delivery organisation that was losing its senior engineers, built a mental model of what actually existed rather than what the architecture diagram claimed, and rebuilt structure and priorities around reality. Velocity returned, client trust returned, and an almost complete exodus stopped at a single departure. At Rohde & Schwarz I resolved an infrastructure blocker engineering had not cracked in over a year, not by writing the fix but by treating it as a product problem, quantifying what it cost per release, and getting the right people in one room. Deployment times went from several days to 20 to 40 minutes.

Integrations are my core, not my adjacency: Product Owner of the integration team at Scheidt & Bachmann, catalogue and API integration for roughly 20,000 users at AOK, and at CLINET I implemented the interface into the incumbent landscape myself, so I know what an integration costs to build and, more importantly for your platform, what it costs to keep alive. Production forensics I do with my own hands, daily, on systems I operate: logs, dashboards and SQL on my own data pipelines and a live marketplace whose partner integrations, payments and messaging, are exactly the kind of dependencies that fail at 2 a.m.

Your posting frames technical issues as revenue and retention problems, and that instinct comes naturally to me: I spent eight years as a startup CFO across three companies I co-founded, so a flaky integration gets priced in churn and support load before it gets prioritised. Hospitality tech I have not worked in, and I will not pretend otherwise; distribution platforms, partner ecosystems and reservation-like flows under load are shapes I know well, and domain specifics are what the first weeks are for.

The practical part: I live in Limassol. No relocation, no visa question, no notice period, and I can be at the office for a conversation any day this week.
