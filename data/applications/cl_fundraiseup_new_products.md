Dear Fundraise Up team,

your posting describes a way of working I did not expect to see in a job ad: written investment theses, decision memos with explicit kill criteria, failed ideas documented decisively, prototypes built by the PM with AI tooling. I work exactly like this, and I can prove it, because I run a product this way today.

My working assumption, written into my CV long before I read your ad, is that most product decisions produce no revenue at all, at least half, maybe three quarters. The only rational response is to treat every decision as a bet: keep it small and cheap enough that being wrong is survivable and fast to detect, define in advance what would count as failure, and file dead ideas as bought information. On construct8, the two-sided marketplace I run in Limassol, that doctrine is operational reality, including the part your posting cares most about: the revenue model. I designed its booking-fee pricing and validated willingness to pay in real sales conversations with contractors, not in surveys.

Revenue models are familiar ground for a second reason: I spent eight years as a startup CFO across three companies I co-founded, one of which, a payments platform, was acquired and is operated by Sparkasse. Take rate, ACV, gross margin and CAC payback are the vocabulary I wrote investor reports in. When your posting asks for a refined, defensible revenue model presented to leadership, I have sat on both sides of that table.

AI-native by default: I build clickable flows, interactive demos and scripted agents myself, daily, in Claude Code and similar tools, and I run a multi-agent framework of my own design in production. For a New Products mandate the consequence is concrete: the cost of testing a thesis collapses, kill criteria get exercised on real artefacts instead of slides, and a dead idea costs a week instead of a quarter.

On enterprise discovery: at EMIL I built a claims module from zero with no specification and no internal precedent, running discovery with a pilot customer's claim specialists, operators and decision-makers, and at Rohde & Schwarz a workshop insight killed our intended workflow within one session; we shipped it alongside an alternative and measured exactly zero clicks on the original. I would rather buy that information in a week than after a year of building.

The practical part: I live in Limassol, so Cyprus remote fits my life as it is. No relocation, no visa, no notice period.
