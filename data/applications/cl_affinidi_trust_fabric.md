Dear Affinidi team,

I operate, daily and at my own expense, the exact problem Trust Fabric solves. I built and run a multi-agent framework in which coordinator, implementer and evaluator agents work a shared queue under guardrails I designed, and when quality drifts I read the traces myself. Verifiable agent identity, policy guardrails and real-time observability are the three things I wish I could have bought instead of built. So when your posting describes moving AI agents from promising proofs of concept to governed production deployments, I do not need the problem explained. I need to know when I can start working on it.

Your posting says this is a role for builders, and asks for a PM who stays close enough to implementation to understand trade-offs. That is how I already work. My approach is AI-first: with AI tooling and a human in the loop I deliver, part-time, what a small team needed full-time a year ago, across requirements, interface design, backend, test automation and operations. I do not write the production code myself, I own it. Running agents this close is precisely what taught me where they break: silent quality drift, confident looping, permissions nobody scoped, cost curves that only appear at volume. Those failure modes are the reason your product category needs to exist, and I can discuss them with your engineers from experience rather than from a briefing.

The identity domain, your stated nice-to-have, is familiar ground rather than new territory. I co-founded Qcrypt, quantum-secure encryption shipped to enterprise customers across a three-layer hardware and software architecture. At Bundesdruckerei I owned secure data structures and identity management for national health programmes under the highest privacy requirements. At Rohde & Schwarz I owned reproducible AI data pipelines with complete provenance and audit trails for safety-critical use, and I have served twice as deputy IT security officer, with governance and data lineage under a recurring audit cycle.

On developer obsession, one story instead of an adjective. The consumers of my platform at Rohde & Schwarz were other teams and external developers. I pushed to join the customer workshops until I was invited, and within one session it was clear their developers worked nothing like we had assumed. We shipped a second, code-heavy workflow next to the intended one and measured which got used: the intended one received exactly zero clicks. Adoption is the only honest metric a developer-facing product has, and I would rather learn that in a week than after a year of building.

I am a German citizen, based in Berlin and Limassol, and available at short notice for the Berlin hybrid model.
