## How I Work

- **I leave my ego at home.** I want the decision, not the last word.
- **I love the problem, never my solution.** Attachment ships the wrong thing beautifully.
- **On greenfield I buy information first.** Cheap experiments make expensive decisions safe(r).
- **I see the whole value chain, not just the backlog.** Defect-to-fix time is a product metric.

Nobody knows which solution the market actually needs. Not us, not the customer, and not the highest-paid opinion in the room. So I treat each product decision as a bet: continuous discovery and cheap prototypes move us toward the answer, and pilot customers verify, or buy us failure information cheaply; that is how products people love get made, not products that merely exist with zero revenues.

Five failure patterns repeat almost everywhere; I am brought in to fix them. **Discovery treated as a short phase:** the wrong thing gets built efficiently. I make discovery continuous and resolve the largest uncertainty first with the cheapest instrument, assuming most bets earn nothing. **Discovery without engineers and designers:** my discovery core is a product designer, the lead engineer and me. **Work arriving as a decree:** I negotiate outcomes, not output, backed by metrics that make decisions falsifiable. **Mercenaries instead of missionaries:** I hand teams the problem and enough context to own it, and repair the structure underneath. **Nobody can say whether it worked:** I define success before we build; our intended R&S workflow lost to a measured alternative: exactly zero clicks. My working mode is AI-first: a disputed flow becomes a clickable prototype in a day; I do not write the production code, I own it.
